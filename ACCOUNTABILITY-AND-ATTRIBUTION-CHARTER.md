# Accountability and Attribution After an Agent Has Acted — Charter

**Status:** Draft, version 0.5 (see Revision History)
**Originating issue:** #1
**Proposer(s) / drafter(s):** Oleh Vasylenko (@OlehVasylenko2000), Management Company Elixir LLC (Ukraine): originating proposer and reviewer. Anton Sokolov (@tyche-dev), Tyche Institute MTÜ: drafter. Co-drafters by part, under the division confirmed on #1 on 4–7 September: @danyka-icam (lineage requirements and cases) and @syedofc (identity and state continuity).

## Summary

Citing evaluation work by the UK AI Security Institute, #1 observes that where agents took unsanctioned actions, establishing what had happened "required an investigation rather than a lookup" (@OlehVasylenko2000, 10 August). That evaluation ran with safeguards disabled, so the point is the reconstruction problem, not the level of risk (#1). This theme states what must be recorded at the moment of action, and what properties that record needs, so that a party who was not present can later establish the execution that performed the act and, where one exists, its supported relation to a logical agent (R8), under whose delegated authority, within what declared scope, and that the record has not been altered since it was made. No persistent logical-agent identity is assumed. The question is asked afterwards, but the requirement binds when the act happens (@OlehVasylenko2000, 15 August), alongside provenance of authority (#5) and runtime verification (#6): "downstream in question, concurrent in requirement" (@cgorlla, 17 August). The theme states properties and classes, not encodings, so that formats can be assessed against it, including formats that do not yet exist.

This charter summarises that text; where the two differ, the posted comments govern.

### 1. What the record carries about the act

- Which agent acted, under whose delegated authority, and within what declared scope (#1, @OlehVasylenko2000). Read with R8: the execution that performed the act, and its supported relation to a logical agent where one exists; no persistent logical-agent identity is assumed (@danyka-icam on #46, 22 October).
- Content that is observed rather than derived from the authored reference: what was done, when, by which endpoint, against which counterparty. A record that carried only verdicts would inherit the coverage limits of whatever policy set was in force (@OlehVasylenko2000, 18 August).
- A conformance verdict where one exists. @cgorlla proposed making the verdict a required field (15 August); @OlehVasylenko2000 proposed that the record be able to carry a verdict rather than require one (15 August); @cgorlla accepted (17 August).
- A reference to the provenance of authority at the moment of the act, which #5 supplies (@pdixon-wpf, 20 August).
- Whether the record has been altered, and whether a party with no relationship to its producer can evaluate it. These are this theme's questions, and provenance answers neither (@OlehVasylenko2000, 15 August; @pdixon-wpf, 20 August).

### 2. Rejection classes (R1–R4)

@tyche-dev offered this half on 24 August, and @OlehVasylenko2000 asked for it the same day ("Please write it"). Text posted 24 August. Reviewed that day by @AntoBud ("Nothing to cut") and @OlehVasylenko2000 ("none of them a cut"), with one drafting point on R4.1.

- **R1 Separability.** A relying party that rejects reports the class of the rejection. A bare accept or reject is not enough. Proposed by @tyche-dev (the failure-class framing on #7); @OlehVasylenko2000 asked for it as a requirement (24 August). Refined: none since posting; @AntoBud: "separability is what makes them useful".
- **R2 The reported reason must be true.** A correct rejection with a misattributed cause does not conform. Proposed by @tyche-dev (24 August). Refined: none since posting; @OlehVasylenko2000 read it as the recursion applied to the rejection report, "the one that produces action against the wrong party" (24 August).
- **R3 Tier 1 classes:** replayed, expired, malformed, out of mandate. Each is decidable from the record and the relying party's own state, and each is distinguishable from the others. Proposed by @tyche-dev (24 August). Refined by @AntoBud, who named what the four share (24 August), and by @OlehVasylenko2000, who placed the classes here and the frozen cases and encodings with the format body (24 August).
- **R4 Tier 2: standing withdrawn.** Every tier-1 check passes, but the grantor would not confer the mandate at the moment the agent acted. The deciding evidence sits with the grantor, so the requirements fall on the record. R4.1: the record declares whether the grantor's continued standing is checkable, and if so where; a record that declares it not checkable conforms, and silence does not (on "and if so where", see Open Question 5). R4.2: a relying party that evaluates standing records which moment it used, the act or the appraisal. R4.3: neither moment is mandated; the choice is visible. Proposed: the class by @AntoBud, the two-tier shape by @tyche-dev (both 24 August). Refined: none since posting; reviewed by @AntoBud (R4.1's visibility is "load-bearing") and @OlehVasylenko2000 (the two tiers are "the part I would keep whatever else changes"), who also raised the drafting point in Open Question 5.
- **R4.4 A carried standing answer is a claim.** Where a record carries the result of a standing check, that result carries what R7 requires. Proposed by @AntoBud (24 August); @OlehVasylenko2000 agreed the same day that it belongs in the text. Clause posted 12 October. Refined: none since.

### 3. Carried claims (R5–R7)

Anything a record carries about the act, beyond the act itself, is a claim made by some party at some moment. @OlehVasylenko2000 raised this recursion for verdicts on 15 August and listed its cases on 1 September: verdict, null assertion, rejection report, standing check, lineage relation, and state. On #18 @OlehVasylenko2000 applied it to a declared operator (31 August). @cgorlla's split of 17 August, agreed by @OlehVasylenko2000 on 18 August, puts the properties of carried claims here and verdict semantics in #6.

- **R5 Negative and null assertions are claims,** subject to R1–R4 as a positive claim is. Proposed by @cgorlla (absence must be attested, 17 August); @OlehVasylenko2000 asked for the null case in the requirements (24 August). Refined: @zhaoxinghua09-cell's distinction between a check that ran and found nothing and a check that did not run (29 September), placed under R5 and R7 on 12 October. Its author accepted that placement for a statement once carried, and argued that a check that did not run asserts no claim, so the record itself should say which checks were in the path (13 October; Open Question 2).
- **R6 Freshness is a relation, not a property.** A record cannot carry freshness. What it can carry is the moment at which its assertion was true, which this theme requires, and the means, if any, of rechecking it, which R4.1 requires. Proposed by @AntoBud (24 August). Refined: none since posting; @OlehVasylenko2000 reviewed it (24 August).
- **R7 Properties any carried claim needs.** R7.1 attribution: the claim names the party that made it, bound so that it cannot be read as anyone else's. R7.2 stated moment: when the claim was made, or the moment it is asserted to be true about. R7.3 tamper-evidence: an integrity check that a party with no relationship to the producer can run. A claim lacking any of the three does not conform. R7 does not make a claim true. Proposed: the recursion by @OlehVasylenko2000 (15 August); the three names follow @OlehVasylenko2000 (25 August) and @danyka-icam (26 August). Clause posted 12 October. Refined: none to the clause; @zhaoxinghua09-cell argued for keeping three properties (13 October), and @cgorlla wrote that "#6 will reference it" (14 October). The evidence basis is an open question.

### 4. Absence and silence

The record keeps three states apart: a reference was established; a bounded search was performed and returned nothing, with its scope and author; nothing was recorded. The third is not a finding. Where authority was never crisply authored, the record does not name a reference because a format expects one; it carries "what was observed rather than what was authorised: what the agent was given, in what form, by whom, and when" (@OlehVasylenko2000 on #9, 20 August). Proposed on #9 by @pdixon-wpf (an absence is a bounded search with an author, and a universal negative carried in its place is "a manufactured certainty of absence") and @OlehVasylenko2000 (the three states), both 21 August. #6 gives the three states verdict-side counterparts in task 3. R5 treats the second state as a claim; R8.3 applies the same split to state, and LR3 and LR5 apply it to lineage (@OlehVasylenko2000, 25 August and 1 September).

### 5. Lineage from representation to action

Co-drafted by @danyka-icam. The record keeps descent, transformed descent, independent reconstruction, examined-but-unresolved, and record silence distinguishable. Similarity and conformance can be recorded, but neither stands in for lineage. An attributable, integrity-protected lineage assertion is not the same as establishing the asserted derivation: R7 makes the assertion checkable as a claim, not true, and where the evidence permits, an examiner may need to reproduce the specified transformation and inspect the action-link evidence (@danyka-icam on #46, 22 October). A NO needs affirmative exclusionary evidence, and missing evidence never yields NO: "A bounded negative observation must not be converted into a universal negative" (FG-TIDA/use-cases#12; @danyka-icam, 13 October). Proposed by @danyka-icam (cases LR1–LR5, 25 August; FG-TIDA/use-cases#12, 24 September), after @pdixon-wpf routed the record side of this question here from PR #15. Refined by @OlehVasylenko2000 in two points (25 August): LR3 and LR5 are different states; and a lineage assertion is a carried claim, which in LR4 the executor makes about its own descent, "the weakest position from which to make it". @danyka-icam "agreed on both refinements" (26 August).

### 6. Identity continuity and effective state (R8)

@syedofc's subsection. R8: a record lets a later relying party establish the act, the execution that performed it, that execution's supported relation to a logical agent where one exists, and the effective state that governed the act; a later state does not stand in for the state at the time of the act (UC7-R2). R8.1 keeps @syedofc's four situations apart. R8.2: where no logical agent identity was assigned, the record does not invent one, and a statement by a named party that none was assigned, an unknown identity and record silence stay distinguishable (UC7-R1). R8.3: a partial reconstruction is a finding with a scope and an author; no evidence is silence. R8.4: state and continuity assertions are carried claims under R7; matching model, runtime or harness references do not establish continuity, and missing continuity evidence establishes neither a different agent nor lineage (UC7-R3). R8.5: what ran an act is not what authorized it.

Proposed by @syedofc (27 August, with four situations, restated as four lifecycle cases on 2 September); @OlehVasylenko2000 called it "a real gap rather than a refinement" (1 September). Refined by @wardduchamps, who tested the persistent-identity assumption (8 September), and by @syedofc, who answered (9 September) and wrote UC7-R1 to UC7-R3 in FG-TIDA/use-cases#7, whose wording R8 takes. R8.5 is @pdixon-wpf's line on that issue: "Running an agent is not authorizing it." Clause posted 12 October for @syedofc to cut, rewrite or replace. On #46 @syedofc confirmed the subsection and the co-drafter attribution, and asked for no further change beyond the distinction below (26 October).

@syedofc asked on #46 that D1 carry one distinction as a record-side requirement (26 October). The record keeps three relations distinguishable: the specific execution that performed the action; its supported relationship to a logical agent, where one was assigned; and the principal or harness context responsible for instantiating that execution, where supported by evidence. They stay distinguishable in particular for ephemeral executions and for deployments without persistent logical-agent identities. An instantiation relationship should not, by itself, establish authorization (compare R8.5). D1 prescribes no identifiers, schemas or identity-binding mechanisms for any of the three. @OlehVasylenko2000 supported carrying the distinction into D1 with attribution to @syedofc (#46, 26 October).

## Out of Scope

- Protocol specification. Encodings, schemas, field names, media types, test vectors and expected-outcome files belong where formats are specified. The thread named IETF work for that (@OlehVasylenko2000, 18 and 24 August). As @OlehVasylenko2000 clarified on #46 (26 October), those references identify relevant adjacent work; they are not a decision assigning exclusive responsibility for future formats to any particular standards body.
- How a relying party reaches a class, and how an execution, an identity, a state or a derivation relation is named or expressed (24 August text; R8; @OlehVasylenko2000, 25 August).
- Model internals, and how any value in a record is computed (#1).
- Replacing logging and audit practice. The question is narrower: what makes a record verifiable by someone who was not there (#1).
- Liability. Determining legal responsibility is out of scope; this theme asks what record makes that determination possible (#1). Governance sits in #3.
- What a conformance verdict asserts, how a verifier checks it, and what an attested absence of evaluation means: #6, task 3.
- Where authority originates, what may be conferred, anchor integrity, and composition and revocation semantics: #5.
- Whether a natural-language goal is a grant at all: #5, with the joint sub-thread #9 (@pdixon-wpf, 20 August).

## Objectives / Deliverables

Objectives:

1. State what an action-time record must be able to carry, and what properties every carried claim needs, so that a party who was not present can establish the execution that performed the act and, where one exists, its supported relation to a logical agent (R8), under whose delegated authority, within what declared scope, and that the record is unaltered.
2. Develop D1 as architectural record-side requirements: the properties and classes a record must support, without prescribing particular encodings, schemas, protocols or conformance vectors (@OlehVasylenko2000 on #46, 26 October), so that they stay "useful for formats that do not yet exist" (@OlehVasylenko2000, 1 September). Of the kinds of work the Working Group charter template of 21 October distinguishes (describe, specify, recommend), D1 is not specification: it states requirements against which formats and protocols can be assessed.
3. Keep the interfaces with #5 and #6 explicit, so that each theme references the other instead of restating it.

Deliverables:

- **D1 Record-side requirements.** One consolidated text of R1–R8, the lineage requirements, the absence states and @syedofc's three-way distinction (section 6), with each clause under its author's name. Requirements only: no encodings, schemas, protocols, identifiers, identity-binding mechanisms or conformance vectors.
- **D2 Cases.** A short mapping of LR1–LR5, FG-TIDA/use-cases#7 and FG-TIDA/use-cases#12 to the requirements they test, as "the material that tests whether a requirement is satisfiable" (@OlehVasylenko2000, 1 September). D2 can draw on the two-case continuity fixture @syedofc posted on FG-TIDA/use-cases#7 at @T-n-Nelson's request (8 October). D2 fixes no conformance vectors. The UC-4 campaigns @T-n-Nelson proposed on those use cases stay separate from D2, as the record-side review stayed separate from the UC12 campaign by agreement with @danyka-icam (FG-TIDA/use-cases#12, 2 October).

How the work runs:

- If the group folds this theme into a Working Group at the Paris meeting, D1 and D2 move into that group's deliverable; as @OlehVasylenko2000 put it on 1 September, "the requirements travel, and the drafting responsibility travels with them".
- A Working Group charter built on the template of 21 October (FG-TIDA/wg-charter-template) carries names and affiliations in its Section 1 only. Clause-level attribution then stays traceable in two places: in D1, where every clause stands under its author's name, and in a linked contribution record: this charter (PR #46), which records who proposed and who refined each clause, and the two requirement comments on #1, which hold the clause text. @OlehVasylenko2000 asked on #46 where this attribution would remain traceable (26 October). The same linked contribution record keeps the Open Questions and the attributed positions recorded under them, alongside clause-level attribution (@OlehVasylenko2000 on #46, 26 October). In the drafter's reading, the other sections map onto the template: Summary to Background and Problem Statement, Objectives to Goals, Scope and Out of Scope to Scope and Non-Goals, Deliverables to Programme of Work and Deliverables, Related Work and Related Themes to Coordination, Dependencies and Liaisons.
- The drafter asks for at least one co-editor for D1. Who co-edits, and the cadence after Paris, are for the group to set.
- Text stands under the name of whoever wrote it (@OlehVasylenko2000, 1 September).

## Open Questions

1. **Evidence basis.** Should a carried claim's evidence basis be a fourth R7 property, or stay with each kind of claim? Raised by @danyka-icam (26 August), in UC7-R4 (@syedofc) and UC12-R6 (@danyka-icam), and put as an open question by @tyche-dev (12 October). @zhaoxinghua09-cell answered: keep three properties, and read the basis as a reference from a claim to something the record carries (13 October). On FG-TIDA/use-cases#12, @danyka-icam keeps UC12-R6's evidence-basis clause "without presenting it as an adopted fourth Theme R7 property" (13 October). R7 stays at three properties until the thread decides.
2. **Checks in the path.** Should the record state which checks were in the path, as a property of the record beside its claims? Raised by @zhaoxinghua09-cell (29 September; 13 October). For the conformance verdict, task 3 on #6 already requires an absence of evaluation to be attested (@cgorlla, 17 August); open is whether the record states, for every check, whether it was in the path.
3. **What attribution binds to.** @wardduchamps suggested the interaction as the anchor always present (8 September). R8 uses the execution that performed the act, and @tyche-dev asked on 12 October whether that meets the need.
4. **R8 wording (answered on #46, 26 October).** The subsection is @syedofc's, and the 12 October text was there for @syedofc to cut, rewrite or replace. The drafter also asked whether @syedofc's three-way split of 9 September (durable accountability identity, execution instance, principal or harness) needs its own clause. @syedofc confirmed the subsection and answered: the distinction between the execution, its supported relationship to a logical agent and the instantiating principal or harness context goes into D1 as a record-side requirement, without prescribing identifiers, schemas or identity-binding mechanisms (section 6). @OlehVasylenko2000 supported this the same day.
5. **R4.1, "and if so where".** Raised by @OlehVasylenko2000 (24 August): the phrase edges toward encoding if it becomes a location field. As a requirement, the record makes "checkable", "not checkable" and "nothing said" distinguishable.
6. **A third UNCLEAR lineage state.** Variant F (similarity only) in FG-TIDA/use-cases#12 is not yet named in UC12-R5. Raised by @tyche-dev (12 October); @danyka-icam proposed it for review as "Endpoints identifiable, relation unestablished", an evidence condition in the record, while a reviewer's UNCLEAR determination on that basis is a scoped assessment (13 October). On #46 (22 October) @danyka-icam supported keeping it: the condition stays separate from a scoped examination that ends unresolved and from record silence, and it does not imply NO.
7. **Issue scope not yet worked:** multi-step delegation, the boundary with law and policy, and record-keeping obligations (scope element 7; @OlehVasylenko2000).
8. **Standalone or within a Working Group.** The minutes of 30 September say the Working Group structure is to be fixed by 30 November, at the Paris meeting. @OlehVasylenko2000 asked on 4 September that the charter wait for it, and on 20 October asked for this draft for joint review before Paris.
9. **Inputs from #5, #36 and PR #28.** Raised by the drafter: should this theme take up "which contextual grant was in force" (@arpitasarker01), a grant's evidence obligation (@solutionsbyjewel) and a change-event record (@zhaoxinghua09-cell, on #36 and PR #28, 15 October), or leave them where they were posted? On the evidence obligation, @OlehVasylenko2000 wrote on #46 (26 October) that a particular grant may impose additional evidence obligations for actions performed under it, but does not itself define the baseline record-side requirements developed under this theme, which preserves the mutual, not sequential, relation between #1 and #5. The question remains open for all three inputs.
10. **Reconstructable or re-computable.** Should the record let a party who holds only the record "arrive at the same result", which needs fixed and identifiable inputs and a deciding function that can be applied from the record alone, or only let a competent party follow what happened? Raised by @zhaoxinghua09-cell (29 September); not yet taken up in the thread. On #46 @OlehVasylenko2000 asked that it stay open (26 October).
11. **Can a deployment meet R8?** What evidence separates two identities that share a model or runtime, and what does partial state reconstruction look like in practice? Asked by @tyche-dev (3 September; again on FG-TIDA/use-cases#7, 12 October). @syedofc offered cases from a running implementation (27 August; 7 September); FG-TIDA/use-cases#7 states that it does not yet answer that request.
12. **Asserted or witnessed moment.** R7.2 accepts the moment the producer states, and R7.3 lets a party with no relationship to the producer check that the record is unaltered, but not when it was written: "a producer can write today a fully conforming record that states an earlier moment, and every check in R7 passes" (@Abracadabrastartup on #46, 10 November). This leaves part of the Summary's aim unmet, that the record has not been altered since it was made. @Abracadabrastartup proposed, in the pattern of R4.1: "R7.2a Asserted or witnessed moment. The record states whether its moment is only asserted by the producer, or bounded by a source independent of the producer (the record existed no later than T, checkable by a party with no relationship to either). Both conform; silence does not. A witnessed moment bounds when the record existed, not that its content is true (R7 does not make a claim true)." The drafter adds two notes for the thread: the witness's bound is itself a carried claim under R7, so R7.1–R7.3 apply to it; and a witness that was requested but not yet obtained leaves the moment asserted. Open: whether R7.2a becomes a clause under R7.2, which would keep R7 at three properties (Open Question 1).
